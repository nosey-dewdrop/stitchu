Couture showcase

Most patterns shared here are quiet, safe garments. These four are not. Each one stacks the engine's full couture vocabulary onto a single dress, the kind of thing a Dior or Chanel atelier draws, to see how far the drafting layers compose. Every pattern piece below is the engine's own output at an EU38 body, not a rendering or a sketch. The number under each look is what the validator said.

Four looks, 52 pattern pieces, zero validator issues. Drafted through the same engine the create page runs. Published 2026-07-17.

Nuit Étoilée

A floor-length evening gown with a bare low-V back, puff sleeves and a bow at the waist.

13 pattern pieces · 3.8 m fabric at 140 cm · validator clean

What combined, and why it drafts.

Four separate opt-in features stack on one dress without a single validator complaint: the puff sleeve raises its own crown, the open-back facing is trued to the cutout it finishes, the bow is a self-lined rectangle caught at a notch, and the vent grows a lapped flap on the center-back seam. Each one is a post-pass that only adds pieces and never touches the outlines the others drew, so they compose freely. That is why the gown drafts in one call: thirteen pieces, no issue in that run's validator log.

Halter Coquette

A cocktail mini with a bare-shoulder halter neck and a three-tier cascading ruffle hem.

halter necklineempire waistbias bindingprincess bodicethree-tier ruffle cascadeA-line mini

10 pattern pieces · 6.7 m fabric at 140 cm · validator clean

What combined, and why it drafts.

A halter is more than a neck shape. The engine drops the whole front by a strap rise, turns the shoulder into a nape closure, replaces the neck facings with one bias binding whose length is measured off the same geometry, and refuses sleeves because a halter cannot host them. On top of that the ruffle hem cascades in three tiers, each tier cut to the hem length times the fullness raised to its tier number. The fabric estimate climbs to 6.7 metres and the geometry is the honest reason why.

Mademoiselle

A tailored shirt-dress with a stand collar, a grown-on button front, long sleeves and a back walking vent.

stand collargrown-on front placketstraight sleevesprincess seamsstraight midi skirtwalking vent

12 pattern pieces · 2.8 m fabric at 140 cm · validator clean

What combined, and why it drafts.

The Chanel discipline is restraint, and the engine builds it structurally. The front placket is a grown-on stand, not an applied band: the center-front edge is pushed out by one button diameter and folds back on itself, so the finished edge is one clean piece with no seam. A button lands exactly on the bust level because that is the anti-gape rule. The stand collar drafts its attach edge dead straight to the neckline length and pulls the center-front in so the band hugs the throat. The whole dress is twelve pieces and 2.8 metres, the leanest of the four.

What combined, and why it drafts.

This is the stress test: every drawable feature the engine owns, requested on one dress at once. Seven opt-in blocks compose on a single draft and the validator stays silent. Seventeen pieces, 8.6 metres of fabric, no issue in that run's validator log. No feature had to be dropped and no combination hit a limit. The takeaway is not the dress, which no one would sew, but the proof that the drafting layers are truly independent: each reads only the finished geometry it needs and adds only its own pieces, so they never fight.
