stitchu / sewing guide / choosing fabric: weight and drape

Which fabric, and why.

The question is never which fabric in the abstract; it is which fabric for THIS shape. Two levers decide it, weight and drape, and one rule ties them to the pattern.

The one rule.

A silhouette that STANDS AWAY from the body wants a fabric that holds its shape: crisp, medium weight. A silhouette that FALLS CLOSE to the body wants a fabric that drapes: fluid, light weight. Gathers and full skirts are the middle case, a soft-medium with body but movement.

Weight (gramaj) is how heavy the cloth is, in grams per square metre. Light is about 80 to 140, medium 150 to 250, heavy 250 and up. Drape is how it falls under its own weight, set by weight and weave together.

Standing-away shapes (fitted bodice, A-line, tailored skirt).

Look for a medium, crisp woven that holds its shape. The fitted seams and the stand-away shape read as clean lines only if the cloth holds them; a fluid fabric goes limp and the fitted lines collapse.

trade-offcrisp cloth creases (linen especially) and needs pressing as you go.

Falling-close shapes (drapey dress, half-circle, cowl).

Look for a light, fluid woven that pours. The fall of the cloth IS the design here; a crisp poplin would stand out in a stiff bell instead of flowing close to the body.

whatwhich
familiesviscose or rayon, crepe, cotton lawn
ask forlight weight, fluid, drapes (about 80 to 140 g/m²)
trade-offfluid cloth shifts while you cut and sew, so cut single layer and use a walking foot.

Gathered / babydoll / empire.

Look for a soft-medium woven with body but movement. Gathers and babydoll fullness need enough body to fill out but enough softness to fall; too crisp and they jut like cardboard, too heavy and they sag at the seam.

whatwhich
familiescotton lawn, voile, chambray, soft crepe
ask forlight to medium, soft with a little body
trade-offget the weight wrong and the gathers either stick out or drag the seam down.

Collared / plackets / tailored.

Look for a crisp, medium woven that presses sharp. The collar stand and the placket fold need a fabric that takes a hard press; a soft viscose collar will not stand up.

whatwhich
familiescotton poplin, shirting, linen
ask formedium weight shirting, crisp, presses flat
trade-offinterfacing matters as much as the cloth: fuse a crisp interfacing to the collar and placket.

Knit fabric is the exception: a stable ponte for structure, a soft jersey for a close fit. The pattern is drafted with less ease because the cloth stretches, and it needs a ballpoint needle and a stretch or zigzag stitch.
