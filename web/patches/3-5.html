The engine now drafts a peplum.

2026-07-17
  • A peplum is the flared flounce that hangs from a fitted waist and ripples out over the hip. It is not gathered: it is cut as a flat ring of fabric, so its inner curve equals your waist and its longer outer curve falls into soft waves on its own. The engine now drafts that ring as its own piece. A full circle gives the fullest flare, a half circle a softer one, and a pointed version drops to handkerchief points at the hem. The curved inner edge is trued to the finished waist so it sews straight onto the bodice with nothing to ease in.
full patterns 29 of 54 to 31 of 54 · both peplum tops now complete

Only the full, half and pointed circular peplum is drawn; a pleated, gathered or draped peplum is a different construction and is still reported as missing rather than faked. The base draft is byte-identical with the peplum off, so no existing pattern changed. 20 engine tests pass, including a new check that measures the inner waist arc back and confirms it equals the finished waist to a hundredth of a millimetre. 20110 web drafts and 37800 vocabulary drafts pass with zero failures, and the golden reference is unchanged to the millionth of a millimetre.
