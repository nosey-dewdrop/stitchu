stitchu / style library

The style library.

Every style below is drafted by the same engine, real pattern-cutting geometry computed against your seven measurements, validated on a 70,200-draft matrix, seams held to 0.00 mm. Each page shows the actual drafting numbers, straight from the engine's formula spec.

Necklines

Sweetheart necklineThe heart-shaped front neckline, a steep centre-front tangent so the mirrored halves meet in a cleft, and a curve that arcs above the chord to form the bust lobe. Halter necklineA halter is more than a neck shape: no shoulder seam, a strap that closes at the nape, a low back, and sleeves are physically impossible, so the engine refuses them honestly. V-neckThe deepest of the classic neckline family. Scoop necklineThe round, open everyday neckline. Square necklineStraight edges, square corner. Boat necklineWide and shallow, the boat neck stretches toward the shoulders instead of dropping toward the bust. Crew necklineThe default.

Skirt styles

A-line skirtThe classic flared skirt, drafted from your waist and hip with percent ease, never a fixed-centimetre guess. Straight skirtThe pencil silhouette: the A-line block with the flare set to zero, so everything hangs from how well the darts fit, and those are drafted, not averaged. Gathered skirtA rectangle drawn up to your waist. Half-circle skirtReal circle-skirt maths: the waist becomes the inner arc of a ring. Pleated skirtKnife pleats with the fold lines drawn in pairs, fold on the second line, bring it to the first, so pleating is mechanical, not eyeballed.

Sleeves

Balloon sleeveA set-in sleeve that balloons at the hem and gathers into its own drafted cuff, the cuff sized from your arm, not a standard. Puff / gathered sleeve capThe classic slash-and-spread, done by the engine: fullness goes into the crown only, the underarm still matches the armhole 1:1, and the surplus is gathered in.

Details & closures

Keyhole cut-outA teardrop opening below the front neckline, mirrored on the centre-front fold, with the facing piece that finishes it drawn for you. Hem ruffle (single + tiered)The ruffle strip is measured off YOUR drafted hem, per skirt style, then cut in printable segments. Front button placketThe couture front closure: a grown-on button stand folded back on itself, with the one placement rule that stops gaping: a button must land exactly at bust level. Fabric ties, sashes & bowsA tie is a rectangle, cut wide enough to fold into a self-lined tube, and long enough to actually reach around YOUR body and knot. Collar familyFive collars, one governing constraint: the collar's neck edge is measured off the finished neckline of YOUR draft, so it can never be the wrong length for the garment it sews to. Drawstring, shirring & smockingHere the panel ITSELF gathers, cut wide, drawn up to fit. Open-back cutoutA shaped opening in the back piece, the back-panel sibling of the front keyhole, with the anatomy rule most tutorials skip: the opening never starts at the neck edge.

Construction

Princess seamsThe default shaping. Empire waistlineThe under-bust seam, drafted against the measurement that actually lives there: the empire bodice is suppressed to your underbust girth, not your waist.
