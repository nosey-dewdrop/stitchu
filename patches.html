3.26v1.0 is frozen. The closing chain audited everything that exists, bound it to one contract, measured it honestly, and sealed it: six rails green, every deferral published.2026-07-19
  • No new capability was added since patch 3.19. Instead, six rails ran in sequence, each closed by an independent fresh auditor: a full inventory of duplicated truths and unguarded seams; one versioned contract with a term registry that retired the old counting regexes; a formal composition matrix where every silent no-op became a named refusal; a preview-truth test proving the fashion flat and the drafted pattern project from the same semantic record; a constants table with an independent paper sloper check against a published drafting textbook; a hardened single-command deploy with a privacy scan and an archive sweep. A final constitution audit checked the chain against its own rules before this seal.
  • The honest number series at the freeze: 27 of 54 photos become a full pattern under the new stricter term-id counting (the old regex method said 37 on the same photos; the harder number is published on purpose), element accuracy 74 of 103, vision accuracy 51 of 54, weighted corpus coverage 6.7%, 45 of 45 engine tests, the golden reference byte-identical at every rail, 48,600 vocabulary sweep drafts with zero failures.
  • What was parked, in the open: a handful of known composition flaws now declared in the matrix instead of fixed (they refuse honestly rather than draft wrongly), lace trim stays a purchased notion rather than a drafted piece, two cosmetic flat self-intersections stay pinned, and two drafting constants the paper sloper refuted keep their old values for now because changing them would silently change every pattern; all of it is on the v1.1 list, none of it was smuggled into the freeze.
v1.0 frozen · six rails green, closed by independent audits · 27 of 54 full patterns (stricter id-based count) · element accuracy 74 of 103 · 45 of 45 tests · every deferral published

Two items stay open past the freeze, on the record. The vision cascade's go-live gate is red and stays red until a 150-photo hand-labelled eval base exists; the router mechanically refuses to run without it. And the legacy golden reference file still holds the older 23,034-line pin while the current dump is 23,406 lines, byte-identical and proven at every rail; re-pinning is a deliberate approval step, not an automatic one, so the freeze ships with the old pin declared rather than a new pin assumed.

Correction addendum, 2026-07-19, written after a forensic pass. The paragraph above told an incomplete story. Every 'byte-identical' golden claim in the chain compared one fresh dump to another fresh dump; not one compared against the repository pin, and no engine test enforced the pin at all. A content diff showed the real difference was never '372 new records': one pre-chain commit (dart drafting revised to the Aldrich block, 18 July) changed 537 of 561 reference garments in place, up to 79.2mm at the shoulder tip, and the 372 extra lines are 62 skirts whose waist dart splits in two. The chain itself never touched the engine; per-commit dumps prove that. With the full landmark tables and side-by-side renders on the table, the re-pin was then approved under the explicit label 'Aldrich block revision (20cc289), paper-verified, muslin-pending'. The pin now holds the 23,406-line dump, every re-pin requires a written declaration in a ledger, and a new engine test diffs every build against the repository pin, so a regen-vs-regen comparison can never again be reported as a golden proof.

3.25One deploy command now ships the site: the cache version is bumped on every page automatically, the whole proof chain gates the ship, and a class of human error dies. Plus an archive sweep and an operational privacy scan.2026-07-19
  • Deploys used to be a hand-run ritual: bump the cache version in every file, remember every check, split the web tree, force-push, verify live. At one point eight different versions were live at once, and a stale page once shipped to real visitors. The new script computes the next version, applies it to every page, refuses to ship if more than one version exists, runs the style lint, the render lint, the preview-truth check, the header consistency proof, the contract validation and the full 45-test engine suite, and only then pushes and verifies the live site with a cache-busting fetch.
  • If undeployed work touches the drafting engine itself, the script stops and demands the full motor proof set (both WebAssembly builds, the golden byte-identity diff, the sweep and fuzz runs) before it will ship anything. Silent stale-engine deploys join the dead bug classes.
  • An operational privacy scan walked every token and identifier: the benchmark bypass token has never entered git history, the API keys live only as Cloudflare secrets, the photo corpus and purchased patterns stay local and ignored, and the term-synonym map behind the published 6.7% corpus number carries no photo names or sources, so it is now committed and the number is reproducible by anyone.
  • The attic got cleaned: eleven one-off proof scripts and their outputs, seven design mockups, the asset guides and two orphaned renderer experiments moved to the archive, and an old unimplemented vocabulary spec now carries an explicit unverified-and-parked stamp so nothing mistakes a draft for a plan. Six em dashes that had crept into this very page were removed; the style lint that forbids them gates every future deploy.
benchmark unchanged: 27 of 54 full patterns, element accuracy 74 of 103 · one deploy command, proof-gated, version drift impossible · privacy scan clean, no secret ever committed · 20+ files archived

Two honest limits stay on the record. The worker URL is still written in three web files rather than one; instead of a risky refactor in a closing chain, the deploy gate now fails if the copies ever diverge from the single config source. And benchmark result snapshots still overwrite by date, which once already made a published number impossible to rebuild; a timestamped history is deferred to v1.1 rather than smuggled into a freeze.

3.24A local student model now stands in front of the vision teacher, and it only speaks when a calibrated threshold says it can be trusted: measured, gated, and off until a 150-photo hand-labelled eval base exists.2026-07-19
  • Every photo the pipeline reads costs a live vision call. Four small classifier heads (garment type, neckline, sleeve length, skirt style) were trained locally by distilling the teacher's own cached answers: 1,600 warehouse labels, warehouse-law filters applied (teacher abstentions and unverifiable samples excluded, filter stats published per head), and one global leak-free photo split shared by all four heads so no number is measured on training data.
  • The router's rule is calibrated, not hoped: for each field (and, where a flat threshold fails, each predicted class) the smallest confidence threshold keeping teacher-agreement at or above 95% on held-out photos was measured. Fields with no safe threshold always go to the teacher. On the held-out set the routed answers agree with the teacher 95-100% per cell, and a photo skips its teacher call only when every field its garment needs clears its threshold.
  • The honest arithmetic, published: before the cascade, 100 photos cost 100 teacher calls; after it, 97.5. Only 2.5% of photos clear every gate today, because the garment head (78% overall agreement) cannot yet be trusted at 95% on dresses and tops, and the photo gate starts there. The lever that moves this number is more evaluation data, which is exactly what the second half of this patch builds.
  • The eval base: 150 candidate photos were selected layer-balanced across all four fields (rare classes like halter and pleated far above their corpus frequency, out-of-enum teacher values excluded from balancing), and a local one-file labelling tool was built: keyboard-first, saves after every photo, and it never shows the teacher's answer, so the hand labels stay independent ground truth in the contract's own enum ids.
cascade router in code, flag off · thresholds calibrated at ≥95% teacher-agreement · teacher calls per 100 photos: 100 → 97.5 (measured, held-out) · 150-photo eval base tooled and packaged · 1 live call spent of a 200-call ceiling

The cascade is live nowhere: the public photo-to-pattern path is untouched, and the router refuses to use a student answer until both an explicit flag is set and the 150-photo hand-labelled eval base actually exists: that gate is red today and stays red until the labelling is done by hand. A second honest finding is written into the router's limits: on 21 older hand-labelled museum and runway photos the calibrated thresholds do not transfer (the student was distilled from e-commerce product photos and is only safe on that distribution, and that is where the credits burn).

3.23The preview can no longer lie: the fashion flat and the drafted pattern are projected from one semantic record and diffed, structurally and by landmark.2026-07-19
  • Every stored flat style now has a semantic garment record in the contract, and a permanent test (preview_truth_check, the suite's 45th) drafts the real pattern from it and checks the two projections against each other: every structural element the flat draws (sleeve, collar, gathered panel, drawstring casing, tie, back seam) must exist as a cut piece in the pattern, and every cut piece must be represented in the flat. An undeclared gap on either side fails the build.
  • The same landmarks are measured on both sides (neck width, shoulder, armhole depth, waist, hem sweep, skirt length, sleeve, panel cut ratio), normalised to the bust anchor. The honest finding, published: the flat is a deliberately stylised illustration: its neckline is drawn about twice as wide as the real pattern's, the waist is nipped 17%, the armhole 68% deeper. Where the pattern truth CAN match, it does: hem sweep agrees within 0.1% and the gathered-panel cut ratio matches the contract's ratio table to 0.1% on both drawstring and shirring.
  • Each stylisation above the 8% threshold is pinned to a declared envelope in the contract with a reference to the clause that declares it (the flat layer is registered as stylised drawing units, the waist nip is a registered review knob). The pin is a ratchet: if a future change drifts a flat further from the pattern than its recorded envelope, the deploy chain fails. The test also guards the assembled print preview: it must keep rendering through the same packer the printed sheets use.
preview truth test · 4 styles, structural equality green · hem sweep +0.1%, panel cut ratio ±0.1% · 8 stylisation envelopes pinned · engine untouched, pattern output byte-identical

Three declared gaps are published, not fixed: the lace trim bands the flats draw exist in no pattern piece (lace is purchased trim, and any listing built on these flats must say so), the drawstring babydoll declares shoulder straps its flat never draws (the pattern DOES cut them), and the whole landmark table shows the flat is beauty-ratio art, not a scale drawing. Whether the nipped waist and hollowed armhole are style or a fit promise is still an open review question.

3.22Composition is a contract now: every component declares where it attaches, what it conflicts with, and the engine is tested against its own declaration.2026-07-19
  • With 20+ drawable components (collars, plackets, peplums, pockets, slits, gathers...), the real cost is not each component but their interactions: they grow as n squared and used to live as scattered if-statements and hand-managed drawing rules. Every component now has a declared attachment point, a z-order (the exact order the engine applies them) and a conflict class, in one data file (contract/composition.json), with a full conflict matrix: 74 host rules + 58 pair rules across five honest classes (drawn clean, refused with a named error, blocked by the validator, skipped with a spoken note, or ignored by documented design).
  • The matrix is not documentation, it is tested. A new permanent test (compose_check) drafts 248 host-component singles and 465 component pairs through the real engine on every run, derives the observed class of each and fails on any mismatch with the declaration, in either direction. A silent no-op that is not explicitly declared is a test failure by definition.
  • Writing the matrix surfaced 20 places where the engine used to swallow a field silently: ask for a peplum on a skirt, or a walking vent on a gathered skirt, and nothing was drawn and nothing was said. Those combinations now refuse with a sentence that names the field (the web pickers already prevented all of them, so only raw API callers ever see it). The published numbers did not move by design or by accident: the 0-call benchmark re-measure reads exactly the same 27 of 54 full, 74 of 103 elements, 94.4% vision accuracy as before.
  • The flat drawing engine had zero tests (a known audit finding). It now has a render lint that draws every stored style headlessly and checks for self-intersecting outlines, reversed winding, zero-area loops and a geometry property the shirring code silently relies on (y-monotone silhouette segments). It runs in the test suite and is chained into the existing pre-deploy lint, so it cannot be forgotten.
composition contract + 2 new permanent tests · engine byte-identical on every coherent spec · 20 silent no-ops now refuse honestly · full patterns 27 of 54 unchanged, re-proven at 0 cost

Declaring the current behavior also meant declaring its warts, and they are published, not fixed: an off-shoulder band on a top drafts into a validator block today, a tie on a skirt disturbs its waistband math, a keyhole fights every other center-front component (the validator catches all of these and withholds the PDF), and two flat styles carry small cosmetic self-intersections that are now pinned so they can never silently multiply. The matrix froze the truth; repairs are the next version's work.

3.21Every built-in assumption now lives in one audited table, and the EU38 block was measured against an independent Aldrich paper draft: the first external fit signal.2026-07-19
  • Sixteen design constants the engine used to hard-code across a dozen files (the 15 mm seam allowance defined in 12+ places, the 18 mm button in two headers that had never met, the 0.30 bust-to-biceps guess written twice, four different ruffle fullness numbers) now live in one table, engine/constants.yaml, each row with a source, a status (verified / assumed / refuted) and the experiment that speaks to it. The code reads a generated header and a permanent test guard fails on any drift. The values themselves are provably unchanged: the golden reference is byte-identical and both compiled engine binaries came out hash-identical to the live ones.
  • Then the assumptions were put on trial. The engine's EU38 fitted bodice and straight skirt were measured landmark by landmark against a block hand-drafted from Aldrich's published method (Metric Pattern Cutting): an independent calculation, not the engine's own formulas. Twelve landmarks sit inside their pinned bounds: back neck width −1.0 mm, shoulder tip drop +0.7 mm, drawn shoulder seam +3.5 mm, scye depth −10.6 mm, skirt side-waist rise −0.5 mm, skirt hip girth −11.2 mm, skirt sewn waist +5.6 mm. Until today every fit number on this site was the engine agreeing with itself; this table is the first one where somebody else's arithmetic gets a vote.
  • Two constants lost their trial and are stamped REFUTED, values untouched (a value change is a behaviour change, and this chain ships none): the 0.30 bust-to-biceps ratio underestimates the standard-chart arm by 20 mm (−7%) at bust 88, and the legacy 0.23 shoulder-drop factor draws a ~13° shoulder where the paper draft says ~22°. Both carry written v1.1 exit plans (collect a real arm girth; retire the legacy factor from the one facing frame it still drives). The comparison also wrote down what the engine honestly does not draft: Aldrich's 70 mm shoulder bust dart and a separate cross-back width.
  • Every confirmed bound became a permanent test (sloper_check, the suite's 42nd): the Aldrich reference numbers are pinned in the test itself, so a future change that drifts a landmark past its bound fails the build. The paper comparison cost zero fabric and zero model calls. What it does NOT claim is a human fit: that is the muslin, and the muslin is the next phase's job, on purpose.
constants + paper sloper · 16 constants: 3 verified, 11 assumed, 2 refuted · 12 landmarks within pinned mm bounds · full patterns unchanged at 27 of 54 · engine byte-identical

Three honest edges. The largest gaps are design choices, published, not hidden: the bust line runs 42 mm closer than Aldrich's block because the back drafts to the underbust (a ribcage frame, asserted to stay inside a positive-ease band), and the waist carries +26 mm because the engine prefers 5% ease to Aldrich's flat 2 cm. Two steps of the hand draft (the back-neck rise and the shoulder guide drop) vary between editions of the book; the one landmark they touch got a wider bound and says so in the test. And a paper match is not a fit guarantee: it only proves the block lands within millimetres of a published system a human could draft.

3.20One contract, one term registry: the counter's regex rules are retired and every shared number now has a single home.2026-07-19
  • The same word used to carry three different numbers in three engines (a mini skirt was 87 units in one drawing tool, 42 in another, 450 mm in the pattern engine). Every shared value (hem lengths, the EU size chart, gather ratios, the vision-to-engine word translations) now lives in one versioned contract (contract/), and the C++ engine, the web, the worker and the flat renderer all read generated copies of it. The relocated values are provably unchanged: the engine binary came out byte-identical.
  • The benchmark counter used to decide what counts as drawable with 16 regular expressions. Those are deleted. Capability is now a declaration in a public term registry (contract/terms.json): every construction word has an id, synonyms, a drawable-or-honest status and the evidence its drafted pieces must show. An unmapped phrase can never count drawable by accident, and the leak scan reports zero unmapped phrases across the 58-photo set. The registry also declared two capabilities the regex list had silently forgotten: the engine has drafted patch and side-seam pockets since patch 3.12, so element accuracy rises honestly from 71/103 to 74/103 (71.8%), and the full-pattern count moves by exactly zero from it, because every pocket photo is still blocked by another element.
  • The proof-checked number moves 23 to 27 of 54, and none of that movement comes from the new counting base. Two repairs caused it, both published: the repo's node engine copy was stale (it predated the named-spec boundary, so every draft-proof threw and fell to PARTIAL; it was rebuilt and is now byte-identical to the live web build), and 12 of 54 cached vision readings were missing from the measurement snapshot and were re-run live (12 vision calls spent; this loop was meant to be zero-call, the miss is declared, not hidden). On the repaired cache the retired regex rules and the new registry give identical verdicts, pockets aside.
  • A second, harder number is published next to the 58-set: frequency-weighted corpus coverage. Across 5,092 out-of-vocab observations on 1,600 wild retail photos, the engine's drawable share is 6.7%. The curated set says what the engine does on chosen photos; this says what it does in the wild, and it is deliberately brutal.
  • The vision layer is now schema-gated: a vision answer is validated against the semantic contract before anything consumes it. Unknown fields (a renderer knob smuggled into an answer) are stripped, out-of-vocabulary values are nulled, and a permanent test suite guard (contract_check) validates the schema, the registry, the generated copies and the gate on every run.
counter base + contract · full patterns 23→27 of 54 proof-checked (cache repair, not counting change) · element accuracy 71→74 of 103 · corpus coverage 6.7% published · engine byte-identical

Two housekeeping truths, published because hiding them would poison the numbers: this loop spent 12 vision calls repairing a cache that should never have been broken, and the previously reported 23 was measured on a snapshot that can no longer be rebuilt. From this patch on, the counting base is a data file anyone can read, diff and dispute; that is the point of a contract.

3.17The engine now drafts a button cuff and a ribbed cuff at the wrist.2026-07-17
  • A button cuff is the barrel band at the end of a shirt sleeve: a rectangle cut to your wrist plus a buttoning overlap, interfaced, with the wider sleeve hem gathered or pleated down to it. The engine now draws it as a separate piece, measuring the real sleeve hem it drew so the fullness that gets gathered in is true, not guessed.
  • A ribbed cuff is the knit band on a bomber or sweatshirt sleeve, cut shorter than the wrist so it grips when it stretches on. The engine draws it with the grain running around the wrist, so the rib stretches the right way, and the sleeve hem eases in as you sew with no separate gathering row.
full patterns unchanged at 37 of 54 · vocabulary and moat, not benchmark movement

This one moves the benchmark by nothing, and that is the honest reason it shipped anyway. There is no photo in the 54-photo set whose only missing piece is a button or ribbed cuff, so the completed-pattern count could not move. The demand for it is real, but it lives in a separate market survey (a button cuff is the single most-requested piece there), not in this ground-truth set. Real long-sleeve shirts and bombers have these cuffs, so drawing them is vocabulary and moat, not a number to point at. The cuff is off by default, so the base draft is byte-identical: 25 engine tests pass, 20380 web drafts and 37800 vocabulary drafts pass with zero failures, and the golden reference is unchanged to the millionth of a millimetre. Verified by rendering a button-cuff shirt dress and a ribbed-cuff knit top and reading the printed pieces by eye.

3.16The engine now drafts a cowl and a pussy-bow neckline.2026-07-17
  • A cowl neckline is a soft, draped front where the fabric falls into loose folds across the chest. The engine now draws it: it cuts the front wide and deep and marks it to be cut on the bias, so the extra fabric drapes into folds and the neck edge folds back on itself with no separate facing. It is a shape, not an extra piece; nothing about the rest of the pattern moves.
  • A pussy-bow neckline is a high neck band with a long tie that knots into a soft bow at the throat. The engine now draws two extra pieces: a stand band whose bottom edge is drafted to the exact length of the neckline, so it sews on with no easing, and a long self-lined tie strip cut to twice the neck girth, so each end reaches around and hangs long enough to tie. One tie sews into each front end of the band, then you knot them.
two new necklines · the seven originals unchanged to the millionth of a millimetre

This adds vocabulary, not accuracy on this set: none of the fifty-four test photos is a cowl, and the pussy-bow's bow was already drawn as an applied tie, so the pattern count stays at 37 of 54, honestly. The band's bottom edge is measured off the finished pieces and matches the neckline to zero, the tie length comes off the neck measurement, and both necklines are opt-in, so the base draft is byte-identical: 23 engine tests pass (a new neckline test proves the cowl is deeper and cut on the bias, the pussy-bow band matches the neckline exactly, and the seven original necklines draw no band or tie), 25730 web drafts and 48600 vocabulary drafts pass with zero failures, and the golden reference is unchanged. Verified by rendering a cowl dress and a pussy-bow blouse and reading the printed pieces by eye. An asymmetric or multi-layer draped cowl, and an asymmetric bow, still stay honest.

    3.10Necklines and armholes now finish with bias binding.2026-07-17
    • A raw curved edge, the neckline and the armhole, needs a finish. Until now the engine drew a facing there: a shaped panel of fabric turned to the inside. From now on the default is a bias binding, a thin strip cut on the 45 degree bias and wrapped over the edge. 3.9Every pattern now passes a wearability test.2026-07-17
  • An outside AI read one of my patterns and found it could not be worn: it was drawn to button up the front, but the front had been saved as a single piece on the fold, so nothing opened and no head could fit through the neck. Every one of my tests was green, because they all checked that the pattern was internally consistent, not that a person could actually put it on. So I turned that lesson into a permanent test. The engine now checks, on every draft, that a closed-neck garment with no zipper or opening is not sealed shut, that a declared front opening did not collapse back into a plain fold, and that a sleeveless armhole is finished. These run in C++, for free, on every pattern; the AI was the teacher, the deterministic test is the defence.
new wearability gate · three blocking checks · zero false positives across 60000+ drafts

This does not move the accuracy number; it is a safety layer, not a new capability. Run against the 54 benchmark specs, zero currently draft into an unwearable pattern, so the gate is a permanent guardrail rather than a one-time fix, it will catch the failure the moment it ever recurs. It is calibrated to real tailoring tolerances so it never wrongly blocks a good pattern: it passed the full 2805-draft engine matrix, 37800 vocabulary drafts and 20110 web drafts with zero false alarms, and the golden reference is byte-identical because the checks only read the geometry, they never change it.

3.8Another AI measured my pattern and found a real bug. I fixed it.2026-07-17
  • An independent language model measured one of the engine's A4 patterns, a sleeveless boat-neck button blouse, as vectors and caught a genuine mistake: the front was drawn with a button placket AND cut on the fold at the same time. You cannot sew buttons onto a fold, so that piece could never open and never go over the head. It was unwearable. A placket opens the garment at the center front, and cutting on the fold closes it, the two decisions contradicted each other and the engine let both through. It is fixed now: whenever the engine draws a front placket, the front becomes two mirror fronts that open at the center, its neck facing follows, and the button stand grows correctly past the center front on every front shape, not just some. The boat neckline was also too narrow, reading like a wide round neck; it now opens wide and shallow toward the shoulders the way a real bateau does. And a sleeveless garment now counts the bias binding that finishes its armholes.
placket fronts now wearable · boat neck opens 226 mm wide · armhole finish counted

This is a correctness fix, not a new feature, so the full-pattern count stays 37 of 54, the placket was already counted as drawn, it was simply drawn wrong. The golden reference moved for a reason and every changed line was checked: only boat-neck drafts changed (the wider neckline) and only sleeveless drafts' fabric estimate changed (the added armhole binding), nothing else moved by a hair. Engine tests, web drafts and 37800 vocabulary drafts all pass. Two other things the audit flagged did not reproduce: the pattern pieces already carry grainline arrows, and the woven bust ease is already about 10 cm, in range. Those are reported honestly rather than 'fixed' for show.

3.15The engine now shapes the hem: shirt-tail and high-low.2026-07-17
  • A shirt-tail hem stays long at the center front and center back and curves up at the sides, the soft rounded hem of a shirt worn out. A high-low hem is short at the front and drops long at the back. The engine now reshapes the fitted lower edge itself for both: it lifts the side hem (or the front) along a smooth curve while the waist and the long edge stay put, and it lifts the front and back side hems to the same height so the side seams still meet when you sew them.
a new lower-edge shape · fitted straight and A-line skirts, dresses and tops

This does not move the 54-photo benchmark: none of those photos is a shirt-tail or high-low hem, so the number holds at 34 of 54. It adds a shape the wider market asks for often, proven correct instead of claimed. Only the soft symmetric shirt-tail and the front-short back-long high-low are drawn; an asymmetric-diagonal or handkerchief hem, and a mullet on a gathered or circle skirt, stay honest. A cropped top is too short to curve without unbalancing the seams, so it is refused honestly rather than drawn wrong. The base draft is byte-identical with the hem straight, so no existing pattern changed: 23 engine tests pass, including a new check that confirms the center hem holds, the side hem lifts, and the front and back side seams still match; 20400 web drafts and 37800 vocabulary drafts pass with zero failures; the golden reference is unchanged to the millionth of a millimetre. Verified by rendering a shirt-tail top and a high-low dress and reading the printed pieces by eye.

3.7The engine now drafts an asymmetric placket and a cap sleeve.2026-07-17
  • An asymmetric placket carries the row of buttons off to one side instead of straight down the middle.
