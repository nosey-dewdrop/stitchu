+//
+// Above skimBaseH the shell obeys the skim envelope (a cone to the shoulder
+// ring); below it, the A-line sweep to the hem. The two met at skimBaseH with
+// NO tangency condition, so the silhouette carried a V corner exactly there:
+// tests/flat_artifact_census.mjs measured 20.5602 deg of tangent break at
+// z=1039.800 on both views, against a 1.0 deg gate. The waist is also the local
+// minimum of the half-width, which is why the corner is at its sharpest there.
+//
+// This is NOT a new algorithm. profile() already rounds the HIP corner the same
+// way (surfacepattern.hpp: "the drafting hip curve"), and the same census
+// measured NO tangent break at the hip. The hip form is the quadratic Bezier
+// with endpoints on the two lines at corner +/- blendMM and the control point AT
+// the corner; its lower line happens to be the constant cylinder, which is why
+// it is written with pC twice. Written for a general lower branch it is
+//     B(t) = (1-t)^2*pTop + 2t(1-t)*pC + t^2*pBot
+// and B'(0), B'(1) reproduce the slopes of the two laws exactly, so the join is
+// G1 on both sides. Feed it pBot = pC and it collapses to the hip's line
+// character for character.
+//
+// The price is stated, not hidden: a Bezier does not pass through its control
+// point, so at h = skimBaseH the shell reads 0.25*(pTop + pBot) + 0.5*pC rather
+// than pC. Rounding a V always adds material; the number that costs the waist
+// ring is measured and recorded in the commit that carries this.
+Section GarmentSurf::effectiveSection(double h, double& dOut) const {

+        skimTopH > 0.0 && skimBaseH > 0.0 && hemScale > 0.0 && blendMM > 0.0;
+    if (!hasWaistCorner || std::fabs(h - skimBaseH) >= blendMM)
+        return unblendedSection(h, dOut);
+

+    const Section sTop = unblendedSection(H + B, dTop);
+    const Section sC = unblendedSection(H, dC);
+    const Section sBot = unblendedSection(H - B, dBot);
