The accuracy benchmark.

Every photo-to-pattern tool promises a pattern that fits. None of them publishes how accurate it is. This is that page, the numbers measured off the drawn pieces, plus an audit of the engine against two published pattern-cutting textbooks.

Last updated 16 Jul 2026 · measured on the 70,200-draft matrix

1. The geometry holds.

These are measured off the DRAWN pieces, not the internal numbers, an audit caught an earlier benchmark reading the intended value instead of the geometry, so now every number below comes from the flattened outline a printer would output.

2. Audited against the textbooks.

An engine can look right and draft wrong. So we put every block, bodice, sleeve, skirt, neckline, armscye, grading, against the two standard references: Winifred Aldrich (Metric Pattern Cutting) and Helen Joseph-Armstrong (Patternmaking for Fashion Design). The rule was strict: change a formula only when BOTH books agree and ours is clearly wrong.

3. Why is this the moat?

Similar tools charge $34–49/mo and publish no accuracy proof at all. The math is the same for everyone; what nobody else does is measure it, audit it against the literature, and put the numbers where you can read them. That, plus drafting on-device so your measurements never leave your browser, is stitchu.
