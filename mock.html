5 Beginner Mistakes When Cutting Fabric

3 min read · by Stitchu Team

Cutting fabric is where your sewing project truly begins, and it's also where many beginners make costly mistakes. Here's what to watch out for.

1. Not washing your fabric first

Fabric shrinks after the first wash. If you cut before washing, your finished garment will be too small. Always pre-wash and iron your fabric before cutting.

2. Ignoring the grain line

The grain line on your pattern should always be parallel to the selvage. Cutting off-grain causes your garment to twist and hang poorly.

3. Using the wrong scissors

Paper scissors and fabric scissors are not the same. Fabric shears have a specific angle and sharpness. Using paper scissors on fabric will give you jagged, uneven edges.

The best investment you can make as a beginner is a good pair of fabric shears and a self-healing cutting mat. Your cuts will be cleaner and your sewing life will be so much easier.

Understanding Fabric Grain Direction

4 min read · by Stitchu Team

Every woven fabric has three grain directions: lengthwise (warp), crosswise (weft), and bias. Understanding these is essential for proper pattern placement.

The lengthwise grain runs parallel to the selvage and has the least stretch. Most pattern pieces are cut on this grain for stability.

The crosswise grain runs perpendicular to the selvage and has slightly more give. Some pattern pieces may use this direction.

The bias runs at a 45-degree angle and has the most stretch. It's used for binding, cowl necklines, and draped garments.

Best Fabrics for Summer Dresses

5 min read · by Stitchu Team

When the heat rises, your fabric choice matters more than ever. Here are the top fabrics for breezy, comfortable summer dresses.

Linen — The king of summer. Breathable, gets softer with every wash, and has that effortlessly chic look. Wrinkles are part of its charm.

Cotton lawn — Lightweight, silky-smooth, drapes beautifully. Perfect for flowy summer dresses.

Rayon/Viscose — Drapes like silk at a fraction of the price. Great for wrap dresses and A-line silhouettes.

Summer Dress

A-Line · V-Neck · Short Sleeve · Midi Length
fabric & materials

🧵 Linen or cotton lawn
📏 2.4 meters (150cm wide)
🪡 Matching thread + invisible zipper
✂ 6 pattern pieces · 1.5cm seam allowance

pattern

▢ calibration square: print this page and measure the square. it should be exactly 3cm x 3cm.

sewing guide
1

Cut your pieces

Pin pattern pieces to fabric following the grain line arrows. Cut with sharp fabric shears. Transfer all notches and markings.

💡

Use pattern weights instead of pins for faster cutting on slippery fabrics.

2

Apply interfacing

Iron fusible interfacing to the wrong side of the facing pieces. This gives the neckline structure and prevents stretching.

3

Sew the darts

Fold each dart right sides together, matching the dart legs. Stitch from the wide end to the point. Press bust darts down, waist darts toward center.

💡

Don't backstitch at the dart point. Tie the thread tails in a knot for a smooth finish.

4

Join front & back bodice

Place front and back bodice pieces right sides together. Sew shoulder seams and side seams at 1.5cm seam allowance. Press seams open.

5

Attach the sleeves

Ease the sleeve cap into the armhole, matching notches. Pin generously, then stitch. Trim and finish the seam with zigzag or overlock.

6

Join the skirt

Sew front and back skirt pieces at side seams. Attach skirt to bodice at waistline, matching side seams and center points.

7

Insert the zipper

Install invisible zipper on the center back seam. Place zipper face-down on the right side of fabric, coil aligned with seam line.

💡

Press the zipper coils open with a warm iron before sewing for a truly invisible finish.

8

Finish neckline & hem

Attach facing to neckline, understitch, and press to inside. Hem the dress by folding 1cm then another 2cm. Press and topstitch or hand-stitch.

~ happy sewing! ~
