3.17

The engine now drafts a button cuff and a ribbed cuff at the wrist.

2026-07-17
  • A button cuff is the barrel band at the end of a shirt sleeve: a rectangle cut to your wrist plus a buttoning overlap, interfaced, with the wider sleeve hem gathered or pleated down to it. The engine now draws it as a separate piece, measuring the real sleeve hem it drew so the fullness that gets gathered in is true, not guessed.
  • A ribbed cuff is the knit band on a bomber or sweatshirt sleeve, cut shorter than the wrist so it grips when it stretches on. The engine draws it with the grain running around the wrist, so the rib stretches the right way, and the sleeve hem eases in as you sew with no separate gathering row.
full patterns unchanged at 37 of 54 · vocabulary and moat, not benchmark movement

This one moves the benchmark by nothing, and that is the honest reason it shipped anyway. There is no photo in the 54-photo set whose only missing piece is a button or ribbed cuff, so the completed-pattern count could not move. The demand for it is real, but it lives in a separate market survey (a button cuff is the single most-requested piece there), not in this ground-truth set. Real long-sleeve shirts and bombers have these cuffs, so drawing them is vocabulary and moat, not a number to point at. The cuff is off by default, so the base draft is unchanged under golden_check: 25 engine tests pass, 20380 web drafts and 37800 vocabulary drafts passed with no failures in that run, and the golden reference is unchanged to the millionth of a millimetre. Verified by rendering a button-cuff shirt dress and a ribbed-cuff knit top and reading the printed pieces by eye.
