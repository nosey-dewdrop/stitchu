3.0

The engine now drafts a back walking slit.

2026-07-16
  • A fitted straight or A-line skirt needs an opening at the back so you can walk. The engine now drafts one: it splits the back on a center-back seam, stitches from the waist down to a marked stop point, and leaves the seam open below it. A walking vent adds a folded-back flap, 40 mm wide with a 45 degree top corner, so the two backs lap closed when you stand; a plain slit finishes the two edges without the flap.
  • The slit only goes where it belongs. A gathered, pleated or half-circle skirt already has room to walk and has no center-back seam to open, so the engine skips the slit there and says so in the guide instead of forcing one. The flap width is measured off the seam line, so it is exactly 40 mm at every point and can never drift.

This is the first engine capability added since the vision chain. It moves the number by two, exactly the two photos whose only missing piece was the back hem slit. The base draft is byte-identical with the slit off, so no existing pattern changed. A front or side slit is still not drawn; only the center-back walking slit is. 18 engine tests pass and the golden reference is unchanged to the millionth of a millimetre.
