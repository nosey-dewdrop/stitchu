3.10

Necklines and armholes now finish with bias binding.

2026-07-17
  • A raw curved edge, the neckline and the armhole, needs a finish. Until now the engine drew a facing there: a shaped panel of fabric turned to the inside. From now on the default is a bias binding, a thin strip cut on the 45 degree bias and wrapped over the edge. Cut on the bias, the strip has the give to hug a curve flat and thin, the way couture finishes a neckline. The engine drafts the strip as its own cut piece, its length trued to the exact edge it binds, and lists the binding metres in the cutting list. A real collar, a shirt or stand collar that sits up on the fabric, still gets its own collar piece on a faced neck; bias is for open, collarless necks and for sleeveless armholes.
bias binding is the default neck + armhole finish · trued strip pieces · facing kept as an opt-in and for real collars

This is a deliberate change to the default, so the golden reference was re-pinned to the facing finish, which proves the old output still draws exactly, byte for byte, when you opt back into a facing. A new bias check proves the strip length equals the drafted edge circumference to a hundredth of a millimetre, that a real collar keeps its faced neck, and that a sleeveless armhole gets its binding. 21 engine tests pass, 20260 web drafts and 37800 vocabulary drafts pass with zero failures. A very sharp inner corner, a true notched or sailor collar, is not a bias case and stays honest rather than faked.
