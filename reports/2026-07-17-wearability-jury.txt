STITCHU — CUSTOMER WEARABILITY JURY
2026-07-17 — "Would Damla actually wear this? Would she take THIS to the tailor, pay money, and wear it?"

This is NOT a technical check. Every pattern below was rasterized (technical flat + on-figure croquis)
and judged with a shopper's eye — a harsh, honest fashion critic, the Kate Middleton test:
is it a real, chic, feminine, wearable garment with real closures and details — or a lifeless technical shape?

Verdict scale:
  WEAR IT   = real, chic, take-to-tailor-ready. A stylish woman would choose it.
  NEEDS WORK = has potential but one specific flaw stops it being wearable/chic (flaw named).
  WEAK       = a lifeless / basic shape almost nobody would choose. Reason named.

========================================================================
FIX FIRST  (has potential — the ONE change that makes it wearable/chic)
========================================================================

- Empire / gathered-bust / drawstring babydoll mini dresses (the empire family):
  The silhouette is lovely but the bodice above the empire seam is a plain sleeveless slab.
  FIX: add a small puff or flutter sleeve AND either a square/sweetheart neckline or a shirred bust panel.
  Right now three near-identical empire dresses all read "blank tank on top of a pretty skirt."

- Open-back tie-back mini dress:
  Gorgeous FROM BEHIND (open back + tie + flare) but the FRONT is a dead-plain boat-neck sleeveless slab.
  FIX: give the front a real neckline (square or sweetheart) and a defined waist seam so the front earns
  the drama of the back. A garment can't be all-back.

- Open-back princess mini dress:
  Princess seams + open back are couture bones, but the square front neck is stark and it reads like a
  clinical pinafore/apron.
  FIX: soften the front neckline (scoop or sweetheart) and add thin shoulder detail or a tie so it looks
  designed, not surgical.

- Scoop-neck tank mini dress:
  It's an athleisure tank-dress. Back zip but zero personality.
  FIX: this doesn't need to exist as-is; if kept, add a collar, a button placket, OR pockets — one real
  detail — or it stays forgettable.

- Back-tie shift mini dress:
  All the personality (a back-waist bow) is behind her; walking toward you it's a blank boat-neck sack.
  FIX: bring an interest to the FRONT — a front tie, a square neck, or a button placket. Don't hide the
  whole design on the back.

- Square-neck back-tie babydoll top:
  Boxy, stiff sleeveless woven tank; the square neck is nice but the body is shapeless and dowdy.
  FIX: add a defined waist / gentle A-line and small flutter sleeves. Right now it hangs like a hospital smock.

========================================================================
HONEST OVERALL VERDICT
========================================================================

Of the 16 patterns on the site:

  5  are genuinely WEAR IT — real, chic, complete garments a customer would pay to have sewn.
  8  NEED WORK — a pretty silhouette or one nice feature dragged down by a plain, lifeless element
     (almost always: a bare sleeveless bodice with no neckline, sleeve, or collar interest).
  3  are WEAK — lifeless technical shapes I'd cut (boat-neck linen shell, boat-neck button-down,
     gingham button blouse).

So: only about a THIRD of the catalogue is something a stylish woman would actually choose off a rail.

THE SINGLE MOST COMMON FLAW, by a wide margin:
  the plain sleeveless bodice with a plain high neckline and NO closure-as-a-feature.
  Over and over the engine produces a "tube with bust darts" — a garment you pull on, that has no collar,
  no sleeve, no interesting neckline, and (in the tops) no real closure. That is not couture; it barely
  clears "basic tank." The winners ALL escape this by having sleeves, a collar, or gathering/ruffles.

  Closures are technically present where they matter (dresses have invisible back zips, blouses have
  button plackets) — so the problem is NOT missing closures. The problem is DESIGN INTEREST:
  the necklines are timid (boat/scoop/square, drawn plain and high), there are almost no sleeves on the
  weak pieces, and the bodices are flat slabs. A garment lives or dies on its neckline + sleeve + one
  special detail, and the weak two-thirds have none of the three.

BLUNT TAKEAWAY FOR DAMLA:
  The engine can draft a genuinely sweet dress when the style calls for sleeves + collar + gathering
  (the babydolls prove it). But right now the catalogue is padded with near-duplicate sleeveless slabs
  (three empire tanks, three plain boat/scoop tops) that no shopper would pick. Before taking these to a
  tailor: take the 5 winners, and pick 1-2 of the "fix first" ones AFTER adding a sleeve/neckline.
  Do NOT pay a tailor to sew the three "cut it" shapes — they are the kind of lifeless top you'd never
  actually wear, and sewing them would just prove the pattern, not produce a garment you love.
